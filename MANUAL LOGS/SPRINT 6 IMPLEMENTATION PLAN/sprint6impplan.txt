# Project543 — Sprint 6 Implementation Plan

## Economy & Campaign Model

**Roadmap position:** R4
**Development philosophy:** System-first → dependency-aware → test-first → integration-driven.

Sprint 6 must **not** become a frontend sprint or an integration sprint.

The roadmap explicitly defines R4 as:

> **money, businesses, income, fundraising, risk, campaign actions, saturation and temporary/permanent effects.**

The implementation must therefore build those systems **independently**, with clean contracts for the later Turn System, Election Engine, AI and eventual Integration Slice.

---

# 1. Sprint 6 Mission

Sprint 6 answers one fundamental gameplay question:

> **How does a political party transform scarce economic resources into campaign influence while managing risk, diminishing returns, uncertainty and long-term consequences?**

This is deliberately **not**:

`Money → Buy action → +Support`

Instead, Sprint 6 should establish the machinery for:

```text
ECONOMIC CAPACITY
       ↓
 available money
       ↓
 income / businesses / fundraising
       ↓
 strategic budget allocation
       ↓
 campaign action
       ↓
 reach / influence
       ↓
 saturation + diminishing returns
       ↓
 temporary / permanent effects
       ↓
 risk exposure
       ↓
 future consequences
```

The player should eventually have to make difficult choices.

---

# 2. Sprint 6 Scope

The sprint has eight primary systems.

### S6.1 Money Ledger

### S6.2 Businesses

### S6.3 Income

### S6.4 Fundraising

### S6.5 Risk

### S6.6 Campaign Actions

### S6.7 Saturation

### S6.8 Temporary/Permanent Effects

These are the explicit R4 requirements.

---

# 3. Architectural Principle

Sprint 6 should **not know how the election is ultimately resolved**.

For example, campaign action code should produce something like:

```text
CampaignEffect
    target constituency
    source party
    magnitude
    duration
    risk exposure
    action type
    causal metadata
```

It should **not** directly modify:

```text
votes
seats
winner
election result
```

Those belong to R6.

Likewise, Sprint 6 shouldn't implement the 45-week loop. That's R5.

This separation is extremely important.

---

# 4. Money System

## Objective

Create a deterministic financial ledger.

Every money change must have:

```text
source
amount
turn
reason
transaction type
```

Examples:

```text
INCOME
FUNDRAISING
BUSINESS_REVENUE
CAMPAIGN_SPEND
PENALTY
REFUND
```

No anonymous:

```gdscript
money += 500
```

should exist in the authoritative economic system.

Instead:

```text
record_transaction(...)
```

should be the controlled gateway.

---

# 5. The Double-Entry-Lite Principle

This is one of the ideas I consider genuinely important.

We don't need a full accounting simulation.

But we **should make every economic event auditable**.

For example:

```text
Week 14

Starting cash                 £82,400

Business income               +£7,200
Fundraising                   +£11,000

Manifesto campaign            -£8,000
Rally                         -£5,000

Ending cash                   £87,600
```

This gives us:

* deterministic debugging;
* explainability;
* future save/load reliability;
* AI compatibility;
* economic balance analysis.

And it prevents the classic strategy-game problem where money mysteriously changes.

---

# 6. Businesses

Businesses should not simply be:

```text
business → +£X every turn
```

Instead, a business should have an economic identity.

Conceptually:

```text
Business
├── business_id
├── constituency_id
├── capital/value
├── operating_income
├── volatility
├── political exposure
├── risk exposure
└── status
```

The **R4 scope explicitly requires businesses**, but the roadmap does not specify their detailed numerical model.

Therefore those details should be treated as a Sprint 6 design decision, not silently assumed to be canon.

---

# 7. Income

Income should be deterministic.

A business could produce:

```text
gross_income
× operating_factor
× stability_factor
= net_income
```

But avoid excessive simulation.

The goal is strategic depth, **not financial accounting software**.

The player should ultimately care about:

> “How reliable is this source of money?”

rather than:

> “What is the depreciation schedule of this factory?”

---

# 8. Fundraising

Fundraising is potentially one of Sprint 6's most important strategic systems.

The interesting design is that fundraising should have:

### immediate benefit

but potentially:

### future consequences.

A fundraising action could therefore generate:

```text
cash_now
+
political_relationship_effect
+
risk_exposure
```

This creates an important strategic decision:

> Do I maximize cash today, or preserve strategic freedom later?

---

# 9. The Strategic Budget Constraint

This should become one of the central mathematical properties of the system.

A player should **never be able to optimize every constituency simultaneously**.

Money is the constraint.

Therefore:

```text
Available Capital
      ↓
Strategic Allocation
      ↓
Campaign Opportunities
```

The game becomes fundamentally about **resource allocation under uncertainty**.

That creates genuine strategic depth.

---

# 10. Campaign Actions

R4 explicitly calls for campaign actions.

The architecture should support action types without hard-coding the future election system.

For example:

```text
CampaignAction
├── action_id
├── action_type
├── party_id
├── constituency_id
├── base_cost
├── intensity
├── risk
├── duration
├── saturation_response
└── effects
```

Potential V0.1 action families already preserved by the roadmap's scope include:

* manifestos;
* rallies;
* interviews;
* campaign activity.

The roadmap explicitly preserves manifestos, rallies and interviews in the V0.1 scope.

---

# 11. Cost Should Not Equal Impact

This is a critical balance principle.

We should **not** create:

```text
£1,000 → 1 influence
£10,000 → 10 influence
£100,000 → 100 influence
```

because then optimal play becomes trivial:

> Spend everything everywhere.

Instead:

```text
cost ↑
     ↓
marginal effectiveness ↓
```

This gives money diminishing strategic efficiency.

---

# 12. Saturation

Saturation is explicitly required by R4.

This is an opportunity for one of Sprint 6's most valuable mechanics.

Imagine:

```text
First campaign contact:
high impact

Second:
good impact

Third:
moderate impact

Fourth:
small impact

Fifth:
almost negligible

Sixth:
potential backlash
```

This means:

> **More campaigning is not necessarily better campaigning.**

That immediately creates strategic allocation.

---

# 13. Saturation Should Be Local

Do not make saturation global.

A party campaigning heavily in one constituency should not automatically become saturated everywhere.

Instead:

```text
Party
  ↓
Constituency
  ↓
Action type
  ↓
Saturation
```

Potentially even:

```text
Party
× Constituency
× Action Family
```

This produces a much richer strategic space.

A player might discover:

> “We've exhausted rallies here. Stop spending money on rallies and switch to interviews.”

That is considerably more interesting than a global campaign meter.

---

# 14. Temporary Effects

Temporary effects are effects with explicit duration.

Example:

```text
Rally
→ +campaign influence
→ duration = 2 turns
```

The effect should carry:

```text
start_turn
duration
remaining_duration
magnitude
source
```

No hidden timers.

---

# 15. Permanent Effects

Permanent effects are different.

For example:

```text
long-term organizational presence
reputation
structural relationship
business development
```

The critical distinction:

### Temporary

changes the current strategic state.

### Permanent

changes the future baseline.

This creates compounding strategic decisions.

---

# 16. The Brilliant Mechanic: Strategic Memory

This is one of the few ideas I believe could materially improve Project543.

Campaign actions should leave **causal traces**.

Not merely:

```text
support +0.04
```

but:

```text
Party A
→ Rally
→ Constituency X
→ turn 17
→ cost £4,000
→ generated temporary campaign effect
→ increased exposure
→ partially saturated rally channel
```

This allows later systems to answer:

> **Why is this constituency behaving differently?**

It also becomes incredibly valuable for AI.

---

# 17. Risk

Risk must not simply be:

```text
random chance of failure
```

That would violate one of our most important design principles:

> **Reward skill over luck.**

Instead, risk should be:

```text
exposure
× vulnerability
× action characteristics
```

with deterministic resolution where possible.

Randomness can exist later as a controlled uncertainty mechanism, but it should never dominate strategy.

---

# 18. Risk Budget

A player should effectively manage two resources:

### Money

and

### Risk capacity.

This is potentially a very strong strategic layer.

A player might have:

```text
£120,000 cash

but only moderate tolerance for additional exposure.
```

They then have to decide:

> Do I use safe low-impact campaigning, or attempt an aggressive strategy with greater upside and greater future consequences?

That's strategy.

---

# 19. Risk Should Be Asymmetric

A particularly useful design:

```text
Low-risk action
→ reliable modest outcome

Medium-risk action
→ stronger possible outcome

High-risk action
→ potentially transformative outcome
→ serious downside
```

But crucially:

**the player should understand the risk profile.**

We should never create casino gameplay disguised as strategy.

---

# 20. Deterministic Risk

For V0.1, I recommend:

```text
same state
+
same action
+
same seed/version
=
same resolution
```

This gives reproducibility.

It also allows us to run:

> “What if I had chosen action B?”

later through simulation tooling without introducing nondeterministic debugging nightmares.

---

# 21. Economic Feedback Loop

The deeper architecture becomes:

```text
Businesses
      ↓
Income
      ↓
Available Capital
      ↓
Campaign
      ↓
Risk / Effects
      ↓
Political Position
      ↓
Future Opportunities
      ↓
Economic Capacity
```

That creates an actual **strategic economy**, rather than a money counter.

# 22. No Direct Election Coupling

Sprint 6 should expose campaign effects through an interface.

Something like:

```text
CampaignResolution
```

containing:

```text

effects
```

R6 can later consume those effects.

This keeps R4 independent.

# 25. Testing Strategy

Every system must be independently testable.

### Money tests

* positive income;
* spending;
* insufficient funds;
* exact balance;
* transaction conservation;
* deterministic ledger.

### Business tests

* valid business;
* income generation;
* invalid values;
* inactive business;
* deterministic output.

### Fundraising tests

* cost;
* proceeds;

* risk;
* repeated fundraising.

### Campaign tests

* valid action;
* invalid action;
* cost;

### Saturation tests

* first action;
* repeated action;
* diminishing return;

### Effect tests

* temporary creation;
* expiry;

* stacking rules.

### Risk tests

* low/medium/high profiles;

* exposure accumulation;
* risk limits.

---

# 26. Property Tests

Beyond individual examples, we should test invariants.

For example:

### Money conservation

```text
starting_money
+ income
+ fundraising
- spending
= ending_money
```

### No negative money

unless overdraft is explicitly designed.

### Saturation monotonicity

Repeated identical actions should not unexpectedly become *more* effective without a defined mechanic.

### Temporary expiry

An effect must disappear exactly when its duration ends.

### Permanent persistence

Permanent effects must survive future turns.

### Determinism

Identical state + identical action must produce identical output.

---

# 27. Anti-Exploit Testing

This is critical.

We should deliberately attempt to break the economy.

### Exploit A

Fundraise infinitely.

### Exploit B

Spend money and somehow refund it.

### Exploit C

Stack temporary effects indefinitely.

### Exploit D

Reset saturation.

### Exploit E

Move effects between constituencies.

### Exploit F

Create negative costs.

### Exploit G

Generate money through circular transactions.

### Exploit H

Repeat an action to create exponential impact.

Sprint 6 isn't complete until these are tested.

---

# 28. Balance Framework

We should **not balance around one "ideal" player.**

Instead test strategic archetypes:

### Economic optimizer

Maximize capital.

### Aggressive campaigner

Spend heavily.

### Conservative strategist

Preserve money and risk.

### Regional specialist

Concentrate resources.

### Broad campaigner

Spread resources.

### High-risk strategist

Accept exposure for potential upside.

The system should ensure none becomes universally dominant.

---

# 29. The Most Important Balance Rule

There should be **no universally correct campaign action**.

If:

```text
Rally > Interview > Manifesto
```

under every possible state,

then the system is broken.

Instead:

```text
action effectiveness =
state × constituency × saturation × risk × timing
```

That creates a strategic decision.

---

# 30. Campaign Opportunity Cost

This should become fundamental.

If the player spends:

```text
£20,000
```

here, they cannot spend it elsewhere.

The game should make the opportunity cost meaningful.

That creates:

> **“Where should I campaign?”**

rather than:

> **“Should I campaign?”**

That's a much stronger strategy game.

---

# 31. Regional Strategy

Because Project543 contains **543 constituencies**, economic allocation can eventually produce geographical strategy.

The system should allow:

```text
concentrated investment
```

versus:

```text
national dispersion
```

without directly deciding which is better.

That is exactly the kind of emergent strategic choice we want.

---

# 32. Information Asymmetry

Sprint 6 should not expose every economic consequence perfectly.

The underlying simulation knows everything.

The eventual player interface can selectively reveal:

```text
cost
expected effect
risk profile
saturation state
```

while hiding deeper causal information until appropriate.

This connects beautifully with Sprint 5's information architecture without requiring Sprint 6 to become an integration sprint.

---

# 33. AI Compatibility

Even though AI is R7, Sprint 6 should be built so that AI can call the exact same actions.

Never create:

```text
PlayerCampaignAction
```

and later:

```text
AICampaignAction
```

Instead:

```text
CampaignActionEngine
       ↑
       ├── Player
       └── AI
```

This is crucial.

---

# 34. Save/Load Compatibility

Although save/load isn't the focus of R4, state should be serializable.

Every major state object should have deterministic:

```text
to_dictionary()
from_dictionary()
```

or the project's established serialization equivalent.

This avoids rebuilding the economy architecture during R9/R11.

---

# 35. Versioning

Every deterministic economic calculation should carry:

```text
model_version
config_version
```

This will become extremely useful during balancing.

If we change the formula later:

```text
economy-v0.1
```

becomes:

```text
economy-v0.2
```

and we can distinguish old simulation results from new ones.

---

# 36. Configuration vs Logic

Another major architectural rule:

**Don't bury balance constants throughout GDScript.**

Separate:

```text
logic
```

from:

```text
balance configuration
```

So we can tune:

```text
campaign cost
saturation curve
risk coefficient
income multiplier
fundraising efficiency
effect duration
```

without rewriting the engine.

---

# 37. A Potentially Exceptional Mechanic: Efficiency Frontier

This is one of the few “special” ideas I would actually recommend exploring.

For every campaign investment, calculate:

```text
marginal influence gained
        /
money spent
```

But don't expose this directly to the player as a perfect optimization number.

Internally it gives us a way to identify:

> **where the next £1 is most strategically valuable.**

The player can learn this through experience.

That creates an emergent optimization game.

---

# 38. Another High-Value Mechanic: Saturation Switching

Suppose:

```text
Rallies:
high initial effectiveness
high saturation

Interviews:
moderate effectiveness
low saturation

Manifestos:
slow effect
persistent effect
```

Then an expert player learns:

> Don't keep pushing the same channel.

Instead:

```text
Rally → Rally → Interview → Manifesto
```

This creates strategic sequencing.

Not button spam.

---

# 39. Campaign Sequencing

That leads to a broader idea:

**The order of actions matters.**

For example:

```text
Manifesto
→ Rally
```

could be strategically different from:

```text
Rally
→ Manifesto
```

But Sprint 6 should expose the machinery for this rather than hard-code arbitrary bonuses.

Later political and turn systems can exploit it.

# 43. The Sprint 6 Constitution

If I had to compress the entire sprint into five rules:

> **1. Money is scarce.**

> **2. Spending is an allocation decision, not a button press.**

> **3. More campaigning does not always mean more influence.**

> **4. Risk must create strategy, not gambling.**

> **5. Every campaign consequence must be deterministic, explainable and reusable by future systems.**

## Final Sprint Leader recommendation

I would classify **Economy & Campaign** as one of the project's most strategically important foundational systems.

The roadmap deliberately places it **before** the Turn System, Election Engine and AI. That's correct: those later systems need a robust economic/campaign substrate to operate against.

And I would **not add any “revolutionary” mechanics just for the sake of novelty**. The exceptional ideas above are included only where they reinforce the core objective: **deep tactical decision-making with deterministic, skill-rewarding systems**.

The truly important innovation isn't a gimmick.

It's the combination:

**scarce capital + asymmetric risk + local saturation + temporary/permanent consequences + opportunity cost + deterministic causality.**

If we get those interactions right, the eventual player won't simply ask:

> “What should I click?”

They'll ask:

> **“Where is my next unit of money most strategically valuable, what am I sacrificing by spending it there, how much risk can I absorb, and what position will that leave me in three turns from now?”**

**That is the kind of strategic depth Sprint 6 should manufacture.**
