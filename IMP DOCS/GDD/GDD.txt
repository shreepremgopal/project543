
PROJECT 543
Complete Game Design Document

Version: 0.1
Document Type: Game Design Document
Development Stage: Pre-Production / Prototype Specification
Genre: Turn-Based Political Strategy & Election Simulation
Core Experience: Strategic campaign management across 543 Indian parliamentary constituencies
Campaign Length: 45 weekly turns
Primary Player Goal: Win the largest number of constituencies
Map: 543 clickable parliamentary constituencies of India
Initial Mode: Single-player
Target Platform: TBD
Engine: TBD

1. Executive Summary

Project 543 is a turn-based political strategy game built around a complete interactive map of India's 543 parliamentary constituencies.

The player creates or selects a political party and attempts to win an election over a 45-week campaign.

The player begins with limited resources and must decide how to allocate them between:

Building long-term income
Raising campaign funds
Managing financial risk
Increasing support in constituencies
Selecting appropriate political messaging
Targeting strategically important constituencies

The game does not simulate individual voters.

Instead, every constituency contains a distribution of approximately 25 abstract voter personas. Each persona has different preferences across a set of ideological dimensions.

The player's party also has an ideological profile.

The election engine calculates how attractive each party is to each persona and combines that information with:

Constituency persona composition
Party base support
Campaign actions
Home constituency advantage
Turnout
Other controlled modifiers

At the end of Turn 45, the election is resolved across all 543 constituencies.

Each constituency is won by the party receiving the highest number of votes.

The final result is represented visually by colouring the 543 constituencies according to their winning party.

The party with the highest number of seats wins.

2. Design Vision

Project 543 should answer one simple question:

Can the player make better strategic decisions than their opponents with limited time and resources?

The game should feel less like clicking buttons to increase numbers and more like managing a national campaign.

The player should constantly face decisions such as:

"Do I spend ₹1 lakh trying to flip this constituency, or invest it into a business that will generate money for the next 20 weeks?"

"This constituency is almost winnable. Should I target it?"

"My ideological alignment is poor here. Is campaigning there worth the money?"

"I can raise a lot of money, but my risk is already dangerously high."

The game's strategy emerges from these trade-offs.

3. Core Design Pillars
3.1 The Map Is the Main Game

The 543-constituency map is the primary game interface.

The player should be able to understand the political situation by looking at the map.

The map communicates:

Current support
Competitive constituencies
Campaign activity
Home constituency
Election results
Final seat distribution
3.2 Easy to Understand, Difficult to Master

The player should understand the visible rules quickly.

Complexity should exist underneath the interface.

The player should see:

"My support here is 27%."

rather than:

"My weighted ideological cosine similarity multiplied by three normalization coefficients is..."

The mathematical model may be sophisticated internally, but the player-facing system should remain understandable.

3.3 Money Creates Opportunity Cost

Money should never simply be "spent because the player has it."

Every expenditure should compete with another possible use.

The player can spend money on:

Immediate campaign influence
Long-term businesses
Fundraising-related actions
Future strategic flexibility
3.4 Constituencies Matter More Than National Percentages

Project 543 is fundamentally a seat-winning game.

A party can have significant national support and still lose the election if that support is distributed inefficiently.

The player therefore needs to think about:

"Where are my votes?"

rather than only:

"How many votes do I have?"

3.5 The Election Must Feel Earned

The final result should be explainable.

After the election, the player should be able to inspect why a constituency was won or lost.

This makes the simulation feel like a system rather than random number generation.

4. Player Experience

The intended player fantasy is:

"I am building and managing a political campaign and trying to turn limited resources into a national electoral victory."

The player is not expected to control every detail of politics.

Instead, the player acts as the strategic leader of the party.

7. Ideology System
7.1 Design Decision

Version 0.1 will not use dozens of individual ideological labels.

Instead, the game uses a small number of continuous ideological dimensions.

Recommended dimensions:

Economic Policy
Welfare
Social Policy
Governance
Environment
National Policy

Each dimension is represented internally on a scale from:

-1.0 to +1.0

The player does not need to understand these numbers.

They choose understandable policy positions, and the game converts those selections into the underlying profile.

8. Persona System

Version 0.1 contains 25 abstract voter personas.

These personas are not intended to represent exact real-world demographic groups.

They are gameplay archetypes describing different combinations of political priorities.

Each persona has:

Priority weights
Optional turnout tendency

10. Persona Preference Model

Each persona has an internal preference vector.

Example:

Persona: Economic Growth Focused

Economic Policy:      +0.90
Welfare:              +0.10
Social Policy:        +0.20
Governance:           +0.60
Environment:          +0.20
National Policy:      +0.50


Another persona may have a completely different profile.

This allows the election engine to calculate ideological compatibility mathematically.

11. Party Ideological Alignment

The game calculates the distance between a party's ideological profile and a persona's preference profile.

The result is converted into an Alignment Score from:

0.00 → 1.00

Where:

0.00 = extremely poor alignment
0.50 = neutral/moderate alignment
1.00 = extremely strong alignment

This value is stored internally.

14. Population Model

Each constituency has a population value.

The sum of all constituency populations should equal the selected national population dataset used by the project.

The initial project target is approximately:

1.5 billion total population

However, the actual game dataset should determine the exact total.

The game should not force constituency populations to sum to 1.5 billion artificially if the imported authoritative dataset uses a different value.

15. Home Constituency

The player selects one constituency as their home constituency.

Selection process:

Open national map.
Click constituency.
Display constituency information.
Show confirmation dialog.
Player confirms.
Constituency becomes the player's home constituency.

The home constituency receives:

+2% Base Support

This represents the party's local organizational advantage.

16. Support Model

A major design improvement is to avoid having one confusing "Base Value" variable perform too many jobs.

Version 0.1 will use three concepts:

Base Support

Underlying party strength in a constituency.

Campaign Modifier

Temporary/permanent support generated by campaign activity.

Election Score

The final value used by the election engine.

This separation makes balancing significantly easier.

17. Base Support

Base Support represents how naturally strong a party is in a constituency before active campaign actions.

Base Support is influenced by:

Party/persona compatibility
Existing party strength
Home constituency advantage
Other predefined starting conditions

20. Rally

Example:

Before: 21.0%

22. Campaign Saturation

To prevent infinite spending from producing absurd results, campaign actions should have diminishing effectiveness.

Recommended v0.1 rule:

Repeated actions in the same constituency during a short period become less efficient.

Example:

First Rally:

+1.00%

Second Rally:

+0.80%

Third Rally:

+0.60%

This encourages geographical strategy instead of repeatedly spending money on one constituency.

The exact curve should be configurable.

23. Time System

The campaign consists of:

45 turns

Each turn represents:

1 week

Therefore:

45 weeks of campaigning

At the end of Turn 45, the election occurs.

24. Turn Structure

Each weekly turn follows this sequence:

Phase 1 — Income

The player receives:

Business income
Other recurring income
Phase 2 — Intelligence

The player can inspect the map and constituency information.

Phase 3 — Actions

The player performs their available strategic actions.

Phase 4 — Resolution

The game:

Applies temporary effects
Updates risk
Updates support
Processes business income
Processes other systems
Phase 5 — Next Week

The turn counter advances.

25. Action Economy

To prevent the game from becoming a simple "spend all your money" simulator, Version 0.1 uses two action slots per turn.

The player receives:

2 strategic actions per week

Actions include:

Rally
Interview
Manifesto
Fundraising
Business construction

This creates meaningful opportunity cost.

Example:

Week 12

Action 1 → Rally
Action 2 → Build Hotel


The player cannot perform unlimited actions simply because they have money.

This is a key v0.1 balancing mechanism.

26. Business System

Businesses provide recurring income.

The business system represents the player's ability to build a self-sustaining financial organization.

Businesses are constructed through a dedicated Business screen.

27. Business Catalogue
Business	Construction Cost	Income / Turn
Food Stall	₹20,000	₹2,000
Water Supply	₹50,000	₹5,000
Hotel	₹1,00,000	₹10,000
Farmland	₹5,00,000	₹50,000
Large Business	₹10,00,000	₹1,00,000

These figures are intentionally simple for v0.1.

28. Business Limits

To prevent the business system from becoming an infinite-money exploit, businesses should have a configurable limit.

Recommended initial rule:

Maximum 10 businesses of each type.

This can be changed after playtesting.

Future versions may introduce:

Maintenance costs
Business locations
Business risk
Market fluctuations
Expansion
Employees

None are required for v0.1.

29. Business ROI

All businesses currently provide approximately the same nominal return:

10% of construction cost per turn

This is useful for initial balancing.

However, this means businesses are effectively investment instruments.

The player must decide whether receiving money immediately is more valuable than receiving larger recurring income later.

30. Fundraising System

The player can perform a fundraising action by requesting financial support from businessmen.

The player chooses how much money to request.

The larger the amount raised, the larger the associated risk.

This creates the following trade-off:

More money
    ↓
More campaign capability
    ↓
Higher risk
    ↓
Potential major penalty

31. Fundraising Risk

Risk is represented as a percentage:

0% → 100%

Risk is visible at all times.

Recommended risk formula:

Risk Increase =
Money Raised / 100,000 × 2 percentage points


Example:

Raise ₹50,000:

+1% risk

Raise ₹1,00,000:

+2% risk

Raise ₹5,00,000:

+10% risk

The exact multiplier should be exposed as a balancing parameter.

32. Risk Threshold

When risk becomes greater than:

50%

the party enters a Financial Scandal State.

The penalty is:

-50% party support strength

for:

10 turns

The penalty should affect election strength, not permanently destroy the player's underlying ideology.

33. Risk Recovery

Risk should naturally decline over time.

Recommended rule:

-2 percentage points per turn

provided the player does not perform another high-risk fundraising action.

This gives the player a strategic recovery mechanism.

Risk cannot fall below:

0%

34. Followers

Followers are a secondary resource.

The player begins with:

100 followers

For v0.1, followers should have a limited purpose rather than being artificially inserted into every system.

Recommended use:

Followers determine the effectiveness of certain campaign actions.

For example:

Rallies consume a small amount of organizational capacity.
More followers improve campaign efficiency.
Campaign activity gradually generates additional followers.

However, followers should not directly equal votes.

Followers represent party organization and enthusiasm, not guaranteed electoral support.

35. Election Model

The election model is the most important system in Project 543.

The election engine must evaluate:

Every party × every constituency

at the end of the campaign.

36. Election Calculation

For every constituency:

Step 1 — Determine Voter Population
Eligible Voters =
Population × Eligibility Factor


The Eligibility Factor can be included in the dataset.

Step 2 — Apply Turnout
Total Votes =
Eligible Voters × Turnout

Step 3 — Calculate Party Attractiveness

For every party:

Persona Compatibility =
Σ(Persona Share × Party Alignment with Persona)


This produces a constituency-specific ideological compatibility score.

Step 4 — Combine With Support

A simplified election strength formula:

Election Strength =
Base Support
×
Persona Compatibility
×
Campaign Modifier
×
Risk Modifier

Step 5 — Normalize

For all parties:

Vote Share =
Party Election Strength
/
Total Election Strength

Step 6 — Calculate Votes
Votes =
Vote Share × Total Votes in Constituency

Step 7 — Determine Winner

The party with the highest vote count wins.

37. Election Model Example

Suppose a constituency has:

Total votes = 1,000,000


Three parties compete.

Party A Election Strength = 0.42
Party B Election Strength = 0.35
Party C Election Strength = 0.23


Total:

1.00


Therefore:

Party A = 420,000 votes
Party B = 350,000 votes
Party C = 230,000 votes


Party A wins the constituency.

38. Why This Model Works

This structure produces several desirable strategic behaviours.

Ideological Strength

A party naturally performs better among compatible personas.

Campaigning

Campaign actions improve performance in targeted constituencies.

Money

Money enables more actions.

Businesses

Businesses create long-term financial strength.

Risk

Aggressive fundraising can produce short-term advantages at long-term cost.

Geography

Winning individual constituencies matters more than simply accumulating national support.

42. Victory Condition

The winner is:

The party that wins the most constituencies.

For the 543-seat parliament:

272 seats = majority

Version 0.1 should distinguish:

Largest Party

Party with the highest seat count.

Majority

Party with at least 272 seats.

The initial game victory condition is:

Highest seat count wins.

A future version can introduce coalition formation.

43. Opponent AI

Version 0.1 requires AI-controlled parties.

AI parties should use the same basic systems as the player.

The AI should:

Receive income
Spend money
Campaign
Build businesses
Fundraise
Manage risk
Target constituencies

The AI does not need sophisticated machine learning.

A rule-based strategic AI is preferable for v0.1.

44. AI Strategic Behaviour

Each AI party should have a personality.

Examples:

Aggressive

Targets competitive constituencies heavily.

Economic

Prioritizes businesses and long-term income.

Campaigner

Spends heavily on rallies and interviews.

Safe Player

Avoids high-risk fundraising.

Ideological

Targets constituencies with high ideological compatibility.

This makes opponents feel different without requiring complex AI.

45. AI Target Selection

The AI should calculate:

Potential Seat Value
×
Probability of Winning
÷
Campaign Cost


It should prioritize constituencies where a relatively small investment could potentially change the winner.

This is one of the most important strategic behaviours in the game.

46. The "Swing Constituency" Concept

A constituency becomes strategically valuable when:

The player is close to first place
Another campaign action could change the result
The constituency has sufficient population
The cost of intervention is reasonable

The UI should highlight these opportunities.

Example:

SWING OPPORTUNITY

You are approximately 0.8% behind the current leader.

This creates clear strategic goals for the player.

47. Information and Fog

Version 0.1 should not hide too much information.

Because this is the first version, the player should be able to inspect:

Population
Persona composition
Current support estimate
Leading party
Campaign effects
Competitiveness

This makes it possible to understand whether the game is working.

Future versions can introduce uncertainty and imperfect polling.

A major technical design principle:

The election engine should be deterministic.

Given exactly the same:

Party data
Persona data

Turn

This makes:

Balancing easier
Save/load reliable
Replays possible
Bugs reproducible

57. Randomness

Version 0.1 should use minimal randomness.

Randomness should not decide the election arbitrarily.

If randomness exists, it should represent small uncertainty such as:

Minor turnout variation
Small campaign variation
Minor events

But the majority of the result should be driven by player decisions and game data.

58. Balance Philosophy

The game should avoid dominant strategies.

The following should all be potentially viable:

Aggressive Campaign

Spend heavily and try to flip seats.

Economic Strategy

Build businesses early and become financially powerful later.

Ideological Strategy

Target constituencies where the party naturally aligns well.

Risk Strategy

Raise large amounts of money while carefully managing the risk threshold.

Regional Strategy

Concentrate resources on a limited number of constituencies.

59. Important Balance Rule

The game should reward efficient targeting, not simply total spending.

A player who spends ₹10 lakh intelligently should potentially outperform a player who spends ₹20 lakh randomly.

This is central to the strategy.

60. Early Game

Turns 1–10 should emphasize:

Establishing the party
Selecting strategic constituencies
Building early businesses
Establishing initial support
Learning the map

The player should have limited resources.

61. Mid Game

Turns 11–30 should emphasize:

Expanding influence
Growing income
Choosing swing constituencies
Managing risk
Responding to AI opponents

This should be the main strategic phase.

62. Late Game

Turns 31–45 should emphasize:

Consolidating leads
Flipping close constituencies
Spending accumulated resources
Managing final risks
Preparing for election

At this point, the player should have a clear sense of whether they are competitive.

63. Endgame Pressure

The player should not be able to spend indefinitely without considering time.

A campaign action on Turn 44 is different from the same action on Turn 5.

This is because there are fewer turns remaining for its effects to matter.

Therefore, campaign effects should have a relationship with duration.

64. Campaign Effect Duration

Version 0.1 should distinguish between:

Permanent Support

Long-term change to base support.

Temporary Support

Effect lasts a specified number of turns.

For simplicity:

Rally → permanent small local modifier
Interview → temporary modifier
Manifesto → temporary strategic modifier

This provides useful differentiation.

65. Manifesto Design Philosophy

Manifestos should not simply give:

+5% everywhere.

That would remove strategic depth.

Instead, they should influence specific persona groups.

Example:

Education Expansion

Strong effect:
Education-focused personas

Moderate effect:
Public-service personas

Weak effect:
Other personas


This means the player must consider constituency composition.

73. Testing Requirements

The election engine requires automated testing.

Test cases should include:

Test A — Equal Parties

Two identical parties should produce approximately equal results.

Test B — Perfect Alignment

A party with perfect alignment to a constituency's dominant persona should receive a meaningful advantage.

Test C — No Alignment

A party with extremely poor alignment should not magically dominate without other modifiers.

Test D — Campaign Effect

A rally should produce the defined support increase.

Test E — Home Advantage

Home constituency should receive exactly the intended bonus.

Test F — Turnout

Changing turnout should affect votes without changing underlying support percentages.

Test G — Risk

Crossing the risk threshold should activate the intended penalty.

Test H — Business

Business income should be correctly added every turn.

Test I — 543 Constituencies

All 543 constituencies must resolve.

Test J — Seat Count

Total seats awarded must equal:

543

This should always be true.

74. Critical Data Validation

Before the first complete election simulation, the game must validate:

Number of constituencies = 543

Each constituency:
    Persona sum = 100%

Population >= 0

Turnout between 0% and 100%

Party colours are unique enough to distinguish them

Each party has a valid ideology profile

Each persona has a valid ideology profile

Each constituency has valid geometry

Each constituency has a unique ID


A missing or invalid constituency should produce a development error rather than silently producing a bad election.

75. Performance Requirement

The game must be capable of calculating:

All parties × 543 constituencies × 25 personas

quickly enough that the election result feels immediate or nearly immediate.

Because the mathematical workload is relatively small, this should not require complex optimization initially.

The important requirement is clean architecture.

79. Design Principle: Explainable Simulation

The player should always be able to ask:

"Why did I lose this constituency?"

and receive an understandable answer.

Example:

CONSTITUENCY RESULT

Party Blue won by 18,420 votes.

Main factors:

Persona Alignment       +++
Your Campaign            ++
Party Base Support       +
Opponent Campaign       +++

This is crucial for player trust.

80. Design Principle: No Hidden Magic Numbers

The game should not secretly give unexplained bonuses.

Every major modifier should be discoverable.

If the player's support increases by 1%, the player should know why.

This is particularly important because the game is fundamentally a simulation.

82. The Core Strategic Triangle

The entire economy and campaign system can ultimately be understood as three competing strategies:

             CAMPAIGN
             /       \
            /         \
           /           \
       MONEY --------- TIME


Money allows campaign activity.

Time determines how long investments have to work.

Campaigning converts resources into electoral strength.

Businesses convert money today into money tomorrow.

Fundraising converts future risk into money today.

This triangle is the central economic strategy of Project 543.

83. The Core Political Triangle

The political system can similarly be understood as:

          IDEOLOGY
          /      \
         /        \
        /          \
 PERSONAS -------- CAMPAIGN


Ideology determines natural compatibility.

Personas determine who exists in each constituency.

Campaigning allows the player to overcome or exploit differences in natural support.

Together they determine election performance.

84. The Ultimate Design Goal

Project 543 should create situations where the player looks at the map and thinks:

"I can't win everywhere."

Then:

"But I don't need to."

Then:

"If I win these 20 constituencies, flip these 8, and protect my 15 strongholds, I might cross 272."

That is the strategic experience the game should deliver.

87. Final Senior-Design Recommendation

The biggest mistake at this stage would be to continue adding features.

Do not add more features to v0.1 yet.

The design is now sufficiently complete to move into system specification and prototyping.

The next development document should not be another giant GDD.

The next documents should be small, precise specifications for:

Persona & Ideology Specification
Election Mathematics Specification
Economy & Risk Specification
Turn & Action Specification
Constituency Data Specification
UI/UX Specification
Technical Architecture Specification

These documents should convert this GDD from "what the game is" into "exactly how every system works."

The first of those should be the Persona & Ideology Specification, immediately followed by the Election Mathematics Specification.

Those two systems determine whether the central 543-seat simulation actually works. Once they are mathematically sound, the rest of the game becomes dramatically easier to implement and balance.
